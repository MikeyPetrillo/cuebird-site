Cuebird FAQ

What is Cuebird?

A routine timer for brains that don't feel time. You break a routine (morning, dinner, wind-down) into small timed steps; while it runs, the whole screen is the current step's color and Cuebird moves you along with a chime. Starting is one tap. Nothing scolds you, ever.

Will the alarm really ring if my phone is on silent?

Yes. When you give a routine a start time, Cuebird uses the system's real alarm (the same machinery as the Clock app), so it rings through silent mode and Focus, even with the app closed. On a Mac, Cuebird uses notifications instead, because Macs don't have that alarm system.

I locked my phone mid-routine. Did the timer stop?

No. Cuebird counts time against the clock, not against the app being open. When you come back, you're exactly where the routine really is, and the Lock Screen countdown keeps you posted along the way.

How do I send a routine to someone?

Open the routine and tap the share icon. It travels as a link; when your person taps it, Cuebird opens and offers the routine, steps and colors included. If they don't have Cuebird yet, the link shows them where to get it. No accounts involved.

Do we each have to buy it?

Usually not. Cuebird supports Apple's Family Sharing, so one $0.99 purchase covers your family group. It's a one-time purchase; there is no subscription and nothing to unlock.

What does Cuebird know about me?

Nothing. No account, no analytics, no ads, no trackers, no servers. Your routines and history live on your device and in your own iCloud. The full details are in the privacy policy, which is short on purpose.

New phone. Where are my routines?

They follow your iCloud account. Install Cuebird, and your routines and history arrive on their own. No login, because there are no accounts.

I missed a day. Did I lose my streak?

One off day doesn't wipe it. The streak is there to notice you showing up, not to punish you for resting. Cuebird never shows a score that shames you, and that's a design rule, not an accident.

What are Quick Timer and Ring Me At?

Quick Timer is a bare countdown with the full Cuebird treatment: full screen color, ring, chime. Ring Me At is a one-off alarm ("ring me at 6:30") that can also start a routine when it rings. Both live at the top of the home screen.

Something's wrong, or I have an idea.

Email support@cuebird.net. Bugs, ideas, and bird puns all welcome. Refunds are handled by Apple through reportaproblem.apple.com.
