Cuebird FAQ

What is Cuebird?

A routine timer for brains that don't feel time. You break a routine (morning, dinner, wind-down) into small timed steps; while it runs, the whole screen is the current step's color and Cuebird moves you along with a chime. Starting is one tap. Nothing scolds you, ever.

Will the alarm really ring if my phone is on silent?

Yes. When you give a routine a start time, Cuebird uses the system's real alarm (the same machinery as the Clock app), so it rings through silent mode and Focus, even with the app closed. On a Mac, Cuebird uses notifications instead, because Macs don't have that alarm system.

I locked my phone mid-routine. Did the timer stop?

No. Cuebird counts time against the clock, not against the app being open. When you come back, you're exactly where the routine really is, and the Lock Screen countdown keeps you posted along the way.

How do I send a routine to someone?

Open the routine and tap the share icon. It travels as a link; when your person taps it, Cuebird opens and offers the routine, steps and colors included. If they don't have Cuebird yet, the link shows them where to get it. No accounts involved.

Do we each have to buy it?

Usually not. Cuebird supports Apple's Family Sharing, so one $0.99 purchase covers your family group. It's a one-time purchase; there is no subscription and nothing to unlock.

What does Cuebird know about me?

Nothing. No account, no analytics, no ads, no trackers, no servers. Your routines and history live on your device and in your own iCloud. The full details are in the privacy policy, which is short on purpose.

New phone. Where are my routines?

They follow your iCloud account. Install Cuebird, and your routines and history arrive on their own. No login, because there are no accounts.

I missed a day. Did I lose my streak?

One off day doesn't wipe it. The streak is there to notice you showing up, not to punish you for resting. Cuebird never shows a score that shames you, and that's a design rule, not an accident.

What is a household?

A link between the people you live with. When one of you starts a routine, everyone else gets a gentle offer to jump in, and joining drops you exactly where the others already are rather than back at step one. Declining is silent: nobody is told whether you joined, so there is nothing to explain and nothing to feel bad about.

Does everyone need to buy Cuebird?

Everyone needs the app, but Family Sharing covers up to six family members with one purchase. Outside a family group it is $0.99 each, once.

Can I be in more than one household?

Yes, and you can start more than one too. Your own home, the flat, your mum's Sunday lunch: each one has its own name, its own people and its own routines, and you can join as many more as you are invited to. Routines that came from a household say which one on your list.

Who is in charge of a household?

Whoever started it, and anyone they make an organizer. Tap someone who has joined and you can hand them the same controls, so it is not all on one person. Only the person who started the household can promote people, which is enforced by iCloud rather than by trust.

What happens when an organizer pauses?

Everyone who joined that run pauses with them, and a skip moves everyone along, so nobody is left three steps behind wondering what happened. If you would rather go at your own speed, "Take my own pace" is one tap and your routine simply keeps running. Nobody is told you did it, and you can do it at any point.

How do I invite someone, and how do I leave?

Both live behind the household button on the home screen. Add people opens Apple's own sharing screen, where you can invite someone, remove someone, or stop sharing. Leaving is one tap in the same place, and your copies of the routines stay yours afterwards.

Who can see what I do?

Almost nothing. A start carries the routine's steps, the name you chose for yourself, and the time it started. Nobody learns whether you joined, whether you finished, or how long anything took you. Your history, your timings and your voice recordings never leave your device. It all travels through your own iCloud: Cuebird has no servers and cannot read any of it.

Can I use my Apple Watch?

Yes. The current step, its color and the countdown are on your wrist, and you can pause, resume and skip from there. The countdown keeps running even if your watch loses touch with your phone.

What are Quick Timer and Ring Me At?

Quick Timer is a bare countdown with the full Cuebird treatment: full screen color, ring, chime. Ring Me At is a one-off alarm ("ring me at 6:30") that can also start a routine when it rings. Both live at the top of the home screen.

Something's wrong, or I have an idea.
