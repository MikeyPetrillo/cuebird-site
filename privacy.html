Cuebird Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 24, 2026

The short version

Cuebird collects nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no trackers, and no third-party SDKs. Your routines and your run history never reach us, because there is no "us" to reach. Cuebird runs no servers of its own. Your own routines sync through your private iCloud. Households stay end-to-end encrypted.

Cuebird is published by Cardhavok LLC, doing business as Havok Holdings LLC, a North Carolina company, which is the party responsible for the app (the data controller, in GDPR terms) and can be reached at support@cuebird.net.

What Cuebird stores, and where

Cuebird saves the routines you create and a record of the routines you run (step durations, skips, completions, your streak, and, if you answer the optional check-in at the end of a run, how it left you feeling). This data lives in places that belong to you:

  • On your device, in the app's private storage.
  • In your own iCloud account, via Apple's iCloud key-value storage, so your personal routines survive deleting the app or moving to a new phone, and stay in sync across your devices.
  • In an end-to-end encrypted household space, only if you join a household: what your household shares is encrypted on your device before it leaves, stored in a shared database Apple runs, and readable only by the people in your household. It does not use your own iCloud storage. See "Data shared with others" below.

Personal iCloud sync uses your Apple Account. That data is stored in your personal iCloud container and is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy. The developer of Cuebird cannot see, access, or retrieve it. If you disable iCloud, Cuebird keeps working with on-device storage only; households still use their own encrypted space when you opt into them.

Data shared with others

Nothing is shared unless you choose to share it. Cuebird does not transmit your information to the developer or to any third party.

Two features move data between people, both started by you:

  • Sending a routine creates a link or file containing only that routine's steps. It travels through whatever app you send it with, exactly like text you type. Voice recordings never travel with a shared routine.
  • Households (optional): if you create or join a household, the contents of what you share with it (the routine starts you announce, routines you share, and, while you run one together, the pause, skip and step changes that keep everyone in step) are end-to-end encrypted on your device before they leave. They are stored in a shared database Apple runs, do not use your own iCloud storage, and are readable only by the people in your household. A start includes the routine's steps, the name you chose for yourself, and the start time, all inside that encryption. Old starts are overwritten by newer ones. To route this between the right people, the shared database also holds a small amount of unencrypted technical data: which encrypted records belong to which household, each member's public key, and timestamps. That routing data does not contain your routines, your chosen name, or your run history in readable form, but it does reveal that a household exists and roughly when its members are active, and Apple, as the operator of that database, can see it. Household alerts reach you as push notifications through Apple's push service when another member acts, and the app may be woken briefly in the background to sync; those pushes carry no name or routine title, only a generic "Something new in your household." When you are in the same room, your phones can also stay in step directly over the local network, which never leaves the room. Nobody in the household is told whether you joined a routine, and neither the developer nor anyone else can read the contents of what you share. You can leave any household at any time from the household screen; leaving removes your access, and deleting a household you started removes its shared area for everyone.

One technical note for completeness: once a day the app asks Apple's public App Store catalog whether a newer version of Cuebird exists, so it can offer you the update. The request contains nothing about you or your device, and the developer never sees it.

Permissions Cuebird asks for

  • Alarms (AlarmKit): only to ring the start times you schedule yourself, so a routine can begin on time. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Microphone: only while you record a voice cue for a step, and only if you use that feature. Recordings are saved on your device, never uploaded, and never leave it.
  • Notifications: only for alerts you opt into, like household starts and the optional check-in after a few days away. Household alerts arrive as push notifications through Apple's push service when another member acts, and carry no name or routine title. Focus and Do Not Disturb silence them like anything else.
  • Local network: only while you run a routine together with people in the same room, so your phones can stay in step directly. It is used for nothing else, and it never leaves the room.
  • Calendar (optional, off by default): if you turn on calendar awareness in Settings, Cuebird reads your calendar on your device to warn you when a routine will not finish before your next event. iOS has no read-only calendar permission, so the system asks for full access, but Cuebird only ever reads. Nothing from your calendar is stored, synced, or sent anywhere, and nobody in your household ever sees it.
  • Health (optional, off by default): if you turn on the sleep rhythm line in Settings, Cuebird reads sleep data from Apple Health on your device to show the time you usually fall asleep beside a wind-down routine's schedule. Cuebird never writes to Health. Nothing from Health is stored elsewhere, synced, or sent anywhere, and nobody in your household ever sees it.

Cuebird does not request access to your contacts, photos, location, or camera.

Children

Cuebird collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes its on-device data. To also remove the iCloud copy, turn off iCloud for Cuebird in Settings → [your name] → iCloud, or delete the app's iCloud data from that screen.

For households: leaving a household (on Cuebird's household screen) removes your access to its encrypted shared space. If you created the household, deleting it removes that space and everything in it for everyone. Starts you announced are overwritten by newer ones either way.

Your rights (GDPR and similar laws)

Because Cuebird's developer collects and holds no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct, export, or delete. Your routines and history live only on your devices and in your personal iCloud account, which you control directly. Payments are processed by Apple as the merchant; refunds and the EU 14-day right of withdrawal are handled through Apple. For any privacy question, contact support@cuebird.net.

This website

cuebird.net sets no cookies, runs no analytics or trackers, and loads no third-party resources. That is why there is no cookie banner: there is nothing to consent to. The site is served by Cloudflare, which processes your network address and the address you open, including an invite link's code, to deliver the page. We keep no logs of our own.
